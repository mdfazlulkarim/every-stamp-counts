      How the score works

      Each country you have visited earns points for the door your passport met there. The more paperwork a door needs, the more points it earns, so a higher score means more effort went into the journey.

      DoorWhat it meansPoints

      The Henley Passport Index counts visa free, visa on arrival and electronic travel authorisation equally. This site separates them, because each extra step costs time and money.

      Why this matters

      Whether a traveller needs a visa is decided by the authorities of the destination, often through agreements between states. It says nothing about the traveller.

      For many people, a visa application means fees, bank statements, invitation letters, travel to an embassy that may be in another country, and weeks of waiting, with no promise of approval.

      Two people can take the same trip and meet very different doors. This site makes that difference visible and gives credit for the effort behind every stamp.

      About the data

      Visa rules
      From visa-matrix, which reads the Wikipedia pages on visa requirements and cross-checks each one with Passport Index.
      Official rank
      Henley Passport Index by Henley & Partners, based on IATA data.
      Map
      Natural Earth, through the world-atlas project. Small countries are shown as dots.
      Limits
      The rules shown are current rules, not the rules on the day you travelled. Residence permits and visas you hold for other countries are not counted. If you hold more than one passport, choose the one you travel on most.
      Privacy
      Your choices stay in your browser. Nothing is sent or stored anywhere else.
      Corrections
      Spotted a rule that has changed? Suggest a correction on GitHub.

      Every Stamp Counts is an independent, non-commercial awareness project. Visa rules change often, so always confirm with the destination's authorities before you travel.
